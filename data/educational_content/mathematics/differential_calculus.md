# Differential Calculus - Power Rule

## Section: Power Rule

The power rule is one of the most important rules in differential calculus.
It states that if f(x) = x^n, then the derivative f'(x) = n * x^(n-1).

To understand why differentiating x^2 gives 2x, consider the definition of a derivative
as the slope of the tangent line at a point, computed as the limit of the average
rate of change as the interval shrinks to zero. For f(x) = x^2, expanding
(x+h)^2 - x^2 = 2xh + h^2, dividing by h gives 2x + h, and taking the limit as
h approaches 0 leaves 2x. This matches the power rule: bring the exponent down
as a multiplier, then reduce the exponent by one.

## Section: Common Mistakes

A common mistake is forgetting to reduce the exponent by one, or forgetting to
multiply by the original exponent. Another common error is applying the power
rule to functions that are not simple powers of x, such as forgetting the chain
rule when the base itself is a function of x.

## Section: Worked Examples

Example 1: derivative of x^3 is 3x^2.
Example 2: derivative of x^5 is 5x^4.
Example 3: derivative of a constant (x^0) is 0, since n=0 makes the whole term 0.
