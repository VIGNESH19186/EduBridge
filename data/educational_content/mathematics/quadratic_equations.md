# Algebra - Quadratic Equations

## Section: Standard Form

A quadratic equation has the standard form ax^2 + bx + c = 0, where a is not zero.
Quadratic equations can be solved by factoring, completing the square, or using
the quadratic formula: x = (-b ± sqrt(b^2 - 4ac)) / (2a).

## Section: Factoring Method

To solve x^2 - 5x + 6 = 0 by factoring, find two numbers that multiply to 6 (the
constant term) and add to -5 (the coefficient of x). Those numbers are -2 and -3,
so the equation factors as (x-2)(x-3) = 0, giving solutions x=2 and x=3.

## Section: Common Mistakes

A frequent mistake is sign errors when factoring, or forgetting that a quadratic
can have two, one, or zero real solutions depending on the discriminant
b^2 - 4ac. If the discriminant is negative, there are no real solutions.
